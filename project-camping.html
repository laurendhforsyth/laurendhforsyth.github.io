Camping Co. Website

A responsive e-commerce website for outdoor enthusiasts to browse, order, and purchase high-quality camping gear and accessories, optimized for both desktop and mobile.

Project Overview

Summary: Camping Co. is a responsive e-commerce website for outdoor enthusiasts to browse, order, and purchase high-quality camping gear and accessories, optimized for both desktop and mobile.

My Role: I led the project independently, managing the full UX process from initial research to visual design, prototyping, usability testing, and front-end development.

Timeline: July 26 - August 5th, 2025

Tools: Figma, Google Spreadsheets and Google Slides

Copyright free icon

Problem Statement

Problem Statement: Online shopping for camping gear is often overwhelming due to the sheer number of products and brands available. Users face challenges in identifying where to start, which products are reliable, and how to compare options effectively. Many retailers fail to provide comprehensive product information or detailed specifications in a single, easy-to-navigate platform.

This lack of clarity creates hesitation around making significant purchases, especially when the quality or authenticity of gear is uncertain. Shoppers also want to avoid investing in cheap, disposable equipment that won’t last, but struggle to find trustworthy resources to guide their decisions.

Camping Co. was created to address these issues by offering a simplified, trustworthy, and user-friendly experience that helps users confidently find and purchase the right camping gear—without the overwhelm.

Market Research & A Competitive Analysis

Upon starting market research, I came across 3 websites that had a decent camping section in them: Academy, REI Co-op, and Cabela's (which is owned by Bass Pro Shops). I did a deep dive into each of them to see how they position themselves within the market, and how they talk about themselves. I also did research into the type of products, as well as the quality of said products from a user standpoint, to see what to watch out for, and what people enjoyed. Below is a brief summary of my findings.

With Camping Co., the main objective from a company standpoint was to appeal to users that specifically camp and to contain a wide range of both quality and materials in order to appeal to a wide range. The website that aligned most with those goals was Academy. It had a wide range of quality and prices, making it appeal to a wider audience. However, Academy is not camping-specific.

REI's Co-op was a close second, but their user reviews were more on the downside due to how expensive items were. It was highly recommended to join their membership as well, for futher sale prices. Users were only buying from REI's when items were significantly on sale and only through their membership, due to the high price point of items.

Cabela's is focused on traditional outdoor activities, and camping is one of them. Upon diving into their Camping category however, I found that there were several subcategories that didn't belong within camping. But it did bring to mind a valuable question - while these subcategories didn't directly relate to camping, should we create a main category that contains all the "extras" and items that users might not traditionally think of?

In order to design Camping Co. appropriately and avoid the downfalls that Academy and REI's have, users need to be able to find items within their price range easily, as well as distinct categories without confusing or unnecessary ones.

Wireframes - Desktop

These were the first wireframe sketches I did with a desktop design in mind. I wanted to design using graceful degradation. I started with the homepage, and moved onto various other pages I wanted to include in the prototype. When designing, I didn't fully utilize the space that would be available, hence the multiple iterations of some pages. I did end up swapping a few of the mobile and desktop designs after the initial sketches were done.

Wireframes - Mobile

These were the sketches for the mobile version of the Camping Co. website. Since I had created the desktop version first, I already had an idea of the general layout of the pages, I just had to figure out how to make that layout display better on a mobile device. I held up my own phone during this process, and imagined in my head what the screen I drew out would look like.

User Testing

I conducted some user testing using ChatGPT with the personas I created and the lo-fi mobile and desktop wireframes within Figma.

There were three main issues with the designs:

  1. Product details page missing specifications, multiple images and detailed descriptions.
  2. Homepage could use some sprucing up for deals, inspiration, new arrivals, promotions, etc.
  3. The checkout process user flow needs more pages to give the user an accurate flow.

For the product details page, the only actionable items I needed to change was adding multiple images through a carousel, and replacing "Product Details" with "Specifications". I didn't feel the need to change the homepage design, as these would be held in the image carousel at the top, and within the links directly below the search bar. I made some major changes to the checkout process flow as it was missing a lot of pages. After the checkout process, I added pages for shipping information, billing information, an order summary and an order placed screen. In addition to these, the original desktop lo-fi prototypes were created using a 1440x1024 frame, so for the hi-fi prototype I swapped this out for a more standard PC monitor size of 1920x1080.

Outcome & Impact

The final outcome of designing the Camping Co. website was a response, user-friendly e-commerce experience designed specifically for campers to find and purchase gear that is best for them. I focused the design on clear product organization, having a strong mobile user flow, and giving users the details they need, addressing frustrations found through research using personas. The design would allow users to compare products, navigate easily, and trust the gear they choose to buy, by being a user-centered design.

Lessons Learned

I learned a lot when designing this project. I found that:

  1. I like designing with a mobile-first philosophy in mind. Not only does a mobile-first philosophy make sense for users who are most likely already outdoors, but also forced me to think with a simpler website structure, allowing me to think with a user-centered approach. Mobile-first also makes more sense in today's world, as the vast majority of people already have a mobile device.
  2. Creating a sitemap is much more complex than initially expected. It requires an understanding of content hierarchy, user needs, and the expected user flow between the various pages. In future projects, I will be spending more time on the sitemap in order to make the next steps easier.
  3. Last but not least, the project really allowed me to focus on the user first. Aligning the frustrations and goals of expected users allowed me to think of the structure and what needed to be focused first, making me feel confident in my design choices.
